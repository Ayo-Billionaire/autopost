# How a post is written

Reader: a small business owner, any kind of business. Builder posts may speak to people who build with AI tools.

1. Write the full thought first. Sum it up at the end. Keep only the summary.
2. Plain British English a non native reader understands once. Say it the way a person says it to one other person.
3. 60 to 280 characters. Each paragraph is ONE complete sentence, with a blank line between paragraphs. One to three paragraphs. Join linked ideas with but, because, so or and. No chains of very short sentences. No sentence over 34 words.
4. Line one must make sense alone to a stranger and give a reason to read line two. If line one already holds the whole lesson, the post is one line and that is fine.
5. Name the real thing. Never "the tool", "the check", "the system" when it has a name. Say Make.com, never Make. Explain any term an owner would not know, or leave it out.
6. Every number says what it counts. Numbers and quoted words come only from the seed or page you were given.
7. Motivation and opinion posts hold no number, no story and no "I did". They are a view or an instruction. Tie every one to running a business: customers, follow ups, prices, invoices, staff, promises, finishing, starting.
8. Never invent an event, client, result, quote or figure. Lesson and builder posts say only what the seed says, told as advice or as what happens. Never claim it happened to a client unless the seed says so.
9. Never: dashes or hyphens, bullets, numbered lists, hashtags, links, mentions, emoji, exclamation marks, semicolons, the word source, questions at the end, "a client" (say my client or your client), swearing, hype, promises of money or results, engagement bait, a name of any client, author or speaker.
10. Never politics. Never religion. Not even in passing. If a post touches either, drop it.
11. No forced comparisons. No slogan that would fit any post. No rule of three. No filler.
12. Every post answers "what does the reader do or see differently tomorrow?" If it does not, drop it.
13. Vary the openings. No two posts in a batch start with the same three words. Do not start more than two with "If".

14. Read in/history.txt, the posts already live on this account, before writing. If one of them already makes your point, in any words, drop the draft.
15. A seed about how a vendor's product behaves may be out of date. Tell the owner's lesson in it, or skip it. State a vendor's behaviour as fact only from a page in in/news.json.
